# J45 — M8.5 launch batching in attention, re-measured

J40 built the launch batching, measured it bit-identical with a 22.2% launch cut, and parked it because gpubox said
+0.7% slower at cap 4096 — a loss J40 blamed on the head-major transposes, whose 8-block occupancy J44 has since fixed.
This card re-applies J40's change, puts it behind `JT_ATTN_BATCH=<cap>`, and re-measures on gpubox. **It is opt-in:**
the change is bit-identical by every check available, it cuts 22.2% of a batch-8 run's launches, and on gpubox it is now
2.6% *faster* in TF32 and 2.1% faster in exact mode at cap 4096 instead of 0.7% slower — both gaps, however, are inside
the run-to-run spread, so `WORTH: no` and this card makes no speed claim for gpubox. The prize the item targets is the
shim's per-launch cost, which only the eGPU shows (G-m8probe); this card's job is to show the toggle is harmless.

## 1. The change and the toggle

`notes/J40.wip.patch` applied cleanly to this tree (`git apply --check` was silent) and is the body of the diff;
`src/layer_attn.c` is the only file I touched (179 insertions, 71 deletions).

* **What it does.** The launches around the per-row GEMMs are token-wise (`jt_to_head_major` x4, `jt_kv_expand` x2,
  `jt_from_head_major`, `jt_kv_reduce`), so they are hoisted out of the row loop: one layout pass per **chunk** of
  consecutive rows over the chunk's packed tokens (`[8][ctok][256]`, head h at `h*ctok*256`, a row's slice at
  `+ (off-coff)*256`), then the per-row GEMMs with a `ctok*256` batch stride (leading dimensions and arithmetic
  unchanged), then one way back to token-major. 6 launches per layer in the forward and 11 in the backward instead of
  `6 x rows` and `11 x rows`.
* **The toggle.** `JT_ATTN_BATCH=<cap>`: unset or `0` runs the per-row path (`cap <= 0` makes a chunk exactly one row,
  so the same kernels are called with the same arguments in the same order, and `attn_chunk_max()` keeps the committed
  tree's `c->lmax`-sized blocks); a positive value chunks consecutive rows up to `<cap>` packed tokens and prints one
  start-up line:
  ```
  attn: launch batching, cap 4096
  ```
  The line is what `ab.sh --expect-b '^attn: launch batching'` / `JT_EXPECT` checks (batch rule 9), so a toggle that
  silently did nothing cannot pass. The value is parsed once into a function-scope static and the line is printed the
  first time either mixer runs, `gemm_init()`'s pattern.
* **Arena.** The blocks are sized `min(max(cap, lmax), n)`, i.e. never more than the batch's own token count, so no cap
  can exceed the forward's 10,240 or the backward's 16,384 floats per chunk token (`JT_ARENA_FLOATS_PER_TOKEN` is
  98,304). Measured: peak **11.24 GB on every run**, A and B, every cap, every mode — unchanged.

## 6. The decision

Rule 8's OPT-IN test is "BITEXACT yes, and the best cap is not slower on gpubox than A by more than 1.5% plus 3x the
spread". **BITEXACT is yes at all three caps and with the toggle unset**, and **no cap is slower in either mode** (the
best one is 2.56%/2.11% faster at 4096 on 5 pairs), so the change is committed behind `JT_ATTN_BATCH`, default off, and
`TOGGLE: JT_ATTN_BATCH=4096` names the setting G-m8probe should measure.

**Why 4096 and not 1024 or 2048.** All three are bit-identical and none is measurably slower here, so the tie-break is
what the toggle is *for*: launches avoided. 1024 does not batch the plan's 520-token rows at all (one row per chunk,
−4.4%) — it is the "free but pointless" point J40 already identified; 2048 gives −17.7%; 4096 gives −22.2% (7,953 →
6,189). On gpubox, cap 4096 is also the fastest of the three in both modes. There is no gpubox harm to trade against it
any more, and on the shim the launch count is the only thing that costs what this item targets.

**What this card does not establish.** Whether the launch cut is worth anything *on the shim*. The gain is
`launches x per-launch cost`, and the per-launch cost is a card-only number (J40's bound from the bench's own header:
11-95 µs, i.e. 0.1-0.8% of a 8.94 s shim step for a 22% cut). G-m8probe measures it on the eGPU; if it comes back
negative, `JT_ATTN_BATCH=0` — or unset — is the committed default and costs nothing.
