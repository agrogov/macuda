## 1. Why the measurement this item is built on cannot be made here

`cuda-shim/test/launch_copy_bench.c` (read only, as instructed) runs N cuBLAS launches and times them with N small d2d
copies or memsets interleaved; its header records what the shim's launches cost on a card: **~95 µs per launch** when
the stream also carries copies (a diffusion step: ~5,000 launches, ~128 `cudaMemcpyAsync`, ~11 syncs) and **~11 µs** for
launches-only LLM decode. Both need `TINYNV_HW` + `TINYNV_SOCKET`, i.e. the eGPU card, which standing rule 4 keeps this
card off ("card runs are separate cards named G-*").

The null device cannot stand in for it, and that is documented in the shim's own source, not just observed:
`cuda-shim/libtinynv/src/tinynv.c:1263` takes the `has_pci` branch into `tinynv_exec_run` and otherwise prints
`"tinynv: would launch …"` and returns, and `exec.c:1191` says so in as many words ("NEEDS A REAL DEVICE … there is no
card-free way to capture this"). What the null device *does* give is a **launch count**, which is what §2 uses: it is
the count half of "launches × cost per launch".

So: per-launch cost = quoted from the bench's header (11–95 µs), launch count = measured here, and the product is a
bound, not a measurement. That is the honest shape of this card's evidence.

## 3. What I built, and the trade it makes (the change is parked, not landed)

`src/layer_attn.c` only (151 insertions, 74 deletions, `notes/J40.wip.patch`). The per-row loop's layout ops —
`jt_to_head_major` for q/k/v/o, `jt_kv_expand`/`jt_kv_reduce`, `jt_from_head_major` — are token-wise, so they do not need
a launch per row. The change lays the chunk's head-major blocks out over the chunk's *packed tokens* (`[8][ctok][256]`)
and runs one launch per op per chunk; each row's slice then starts at `h*ctok*256 + (off-coff)*256`, so the per-row
GEMMs' batch stride becomes `ctok*256` instead of `L*256` while their leading dimensions (256) and their arithmetic stay
**exactly** what they were. Per layer that is 6 launches in the forward and 11 in the backward instead of `6×rows` and
`11×rows`. A chunk is capped (`ATTN_CHUNK_TOKENS`) so a chunk is at most `max(cap, lmax)` tokens — a single long row is a
chunk of its own — which keeps the arena's per-token budget (`JT_ARENA_FLOATS_PER_TOKEN`, 98,304) satisfied for every
batch shape that satisfies it today: 10,240 floats per chunk token in the forward (4 blocks + `kv_expand`'s scratch) and
16,384 in the backward, on top of the unchanged `2/3 × 8 × lmax²` slabs.

The cap is the trade, and it is a memory-locality trade, not a launch one: hoisting the blocks makes head h of a row live
`ctok*256` floats away from head h+1 (`4.2 MB` at `ctok = 4096`) instead of adjacent (`0.5 MB` at `L = 520`), and the
per-row attention GEMMs lose some L2 locality for it. That is what the +0.4%/+0.7% in §2's table is, and the ordering
(`1024` free < `2048` +0.4% < `4096` +0.7%) is the signature of it. At `cap = 1024` the batching is free on gpubox but
buys almost nothing, because 2×520-token rows do not fit in 1024 tokens — one row per chunk is the old per-row layout.

The change is **bit-exact**: in exact mode the 20-step `--trace-loss` trace and both `pred-test.jsonl` files are
byte-identical to the committed tree's (§4). So the gpubox delta is pure memory behaviour, and no gate, tolerance or
compute mode was touched to get any of these numbers.

## 6. So what is the change worth on the shim? (a bound, not a measurement)

At the plan's shape a training step is ~3,000–4,000 launches (the 8-row run above is 6,189 launches for one step plus
two dev checks and the recompute forward). With the bench's own per-launch figures:

| per launch | a step's ~3,500 launches | −22% of them | as a share of M7.3's 8.94 s/step |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 11 µs (launches only) | 39 ms | 8.5 ms | **0.1%** |
| 95 µs (launches + copies) | 333 ms | 73 ms | **0.8%** |

Against that, gpubox's measured cost is 8 ms/step (0.7%) — the same order as the whole shim prize, on the platform where
I can see it, and the shim's remaining ~8 s/step is not dispatch at all. A full arm is 3,581 steps, so the best case is
~4 minutes of a 9–10 hour arm. That is not a trade I can defend with the numbers available, and it is exactly the
question the plan told me to settle *first*.

**Best guess** at why the item looks attractive on paper and small in practice: `launch_copy_bench`'s 95 µs comes from a
diffusion step, which interleaves ~128 copies and 11 syncs with its launches; jefftrain's step is launches on one
non-blocking stream with almost no copies after start-up, so it sits nearer the 11 µs, launches-only end — and jefftrain
has an order of magnitude fewer launches per step than that diffusion workload (3–4 K vs 5 K for a *diffusion* step that
does far less arithmetic). The shim's cost is where M7.3 pointed before I started: `tinyblas_gemm_*` throughput (57% of
the launches and, by subtraction, ~7.8 s of the 8.94 s/step).

## 7. What a follow-up needs (if the user wants M8.5 to land)

1. **One G-card run of `launch_copy_bench`** — `launch_copy_bench <N> <K> launches|copies|memsets` at our shape (K =
   never, i.e. `launches`, plus `copies`) — that single number decides the item. If it comes back at ~95 µs, a 22% cut is
   ~0.8% of a step; if it comes back at ~11 µs, the item is dead and the effort belongs in `cuda-shim/`'s GEMM path.
2. If it is worth having, take `notes/J40.wip.patch` and add the one thing that makes the trade positive: **the
   transpose kernels' occupancy** (`kernels/misc.cu`'s `jt_to/from_head_major` launch `jt_grid_rows(heads)` = **8
   blocks for the whole GPU**, J38's measured 7.4% TF32 / 15.8% exact of a step). One block per (head, token-tile) — or a
   flat grid-stride over `rows × 256` — fixes the reason the blocks are far apart in the first place and would pay back
   the 0.4–0.7% many times over. That needs `kernels/misc.cu` + `test/k_misc.c`, i.e. a re-cut with those files in the
   list (no new op, no new reference: `test/k_misc.c`'s existing group gates it).
3. Do not raise the cap past ~2048 while rows are ~520 tokens long: from 2048 to 4096 the launch count improves 17.7% →
   22.2% while gpubox goes +0.4% → +0.7%, so the last 4.5 points of launch reduction are the expensive ones.
