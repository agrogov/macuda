## 1. What was built

The derivation is at the top of `kernels/gdn.cu`'s chunked section and in `gdn.h`; in one paragraph: `g[t,h]` is a
scalar, so unrolling design §3.6 over a chunk of `C = 64` tokens gives `S_i = e^{G_i} S_0 + Σ_{j≤i} e^{G_i-G_j} k_j δ_jᵀ`
with `G_i = Σ_{s≤i} g_s`, the delta system `(I + diag(β)A) Δ = diag(β)(V - diag(e^{G_i}) K S_0)` with
`A[i][j] = (k_i·k_j)e^{G_i-G_j}` (j<i), the output `O = diag(e^{G_i}) Q S_0 + P Δ` with `P[i][j] = (q_i·k_j)e^{G_i-G_j}`
(j≤i), and the exit state `S_out = e^{G_{C-1}} S_0 + KdᵀΔ`, `Kd[j] = k_j e^{G_{C-1}-G_j}`. So a chunk is 7 GEMMs and one
64×64 unit-lower-triangular inverse (`T = (I+L)^{-1}`, one thread per column of the forward substitution), and the only
thing that crosses a chunk boundary is the state.

The backward is the exact derivative of those expressions, with three identities that keep it GEMM-shaped:
`dT = dΔ Xᵀ`, `dX = Tᵀ dΔ`, `dL = -Tᵀ dT Tᵀ` (from `(I+L)T = I`); 16 GEMMs per chunk, plus the forward's 7 because the
backward recomputes the chunk instead of storing every sequence's intermediates. **dg** comes from differentiating the
decay factors themselves: each `e^{G_i-G_j}` and `e^{G_i}` contributes to a per-token cotangent `cG_i`, and
`dg_s = Σ_{i≥s} cG_i` (a reverse cumulative sum inside the chunk) — five groups of terms: A, P, the output's `Q S_0`, the
delta system's `K S_0`, and Kd plus the state carry.

Ragged rows are handled by zero padding: tokens past a row's end get `k = v = q = 0`, `g = 0`, `β = 0`, which makes A, P,
X and Δ exactly zero there and keeps `e^{G_{C-1}}` equal to the last real token's cumulative decay. The one place padding
is not free is a *cotangent*: the state carry's and Kd's factors depend on the last **real** token's `G`, so
`gdn_chunk_dsadd_kernel`/`gdn_chunk_kdbwd_kernel` compute `last = cnt - 1` per block from the device row table (a
`last = C-1` there was a real bug in the prototype, caught by the finite-difference check).

The chunked GEMMs run on `CUBLAS_COMPUTE_32F`, not on `gemm.c`'s run mode: TF32's ~5e-4 mantissa cannot meet this
kernel's 1e-4 gate (plan M3.2). That is also the root cause of the speed result in §3.

Order of work, as the plan's rule 2 requires: the math was written and validated **before** any CUDA — a double-precision
prototype of both directions against a literal sequential double oracle (agreement 1e-15 on L = 1 … 1026, C = 32/64,
D = 64/128, including finite differences on g, β, k, v), then the same code in float against the double oracle (1e-6 …
7e-7), then the CUDA. Two real errors were found that way and not on the GPU: the index swap in `dKd` (which made the whole
multi-chunk `dk`/`dg` wrong, growing with position in the chunk) and the fact that `dL = -Tᵀ dT Tᵀ` is a full matrix whose
*upper* triangle is spurious — only its strictly lower part is the cotangent L has, and feeding the whole thing to dA
over-counts (a standalone 4×4 check of the identity settled it).

Both implementations are kept and both are exercised by `test/test_gdn.c`; `gdn.h` documents why the chunked one is
opt-in.

## 3. The speed change (plan M8 item 2's actual point)

gpubox, RTX 5090 (sm_120), exact fp32, H = 16, one GDN recurrence call (the numbers `test_gdn` prints; backward =
best of 4 warm calls, forward = one warm call):

| shape | sequential | chunked | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| B=8 {1026,520,670,300,811,455,610,702}, N=5094 — **forward** | 1.36 ms | 4.50 ms | **3.3x slower** |
| … — **backward** | 12.53 ms | 9.35 ms | **1.34x faster** |
| … — layer (fwd+bwd) | 13.89 ms | 13.85 ms | 1.003x (a wash) |
| B=8 {520 x 8}, N=4160 — forward | 0.75 ms | 2.32 ms | 3.1x slower |
| … — backward | 7.76 ms | 5.01 ms | **1.55x faster** |
| … — layer (fwd+bwd) | 8.51 ms | 7.33 ms | **1.16x faster** |
| trainer, 20 steps, 256 rows, batch 8, exact (mean of steps 5–20; sequential two reps, chunked three) | 1.145 / 1.146 s/step | 1.205 / 1.206 / 1.208 s/step | **+5.2% per step** |

So: the backward — the expensive half, the memory-bound one 13 GB of `sp` traffic made expensive — is genuinely 1.3–1.55x
faster as GEMMs, and the whole GDN layer is 1.16x faster at the uniform-520 shape. But the trainer is 5.2% slower
(+0.060 s/step, 18 GDN layers), and the crossed sign between "layer 1.16x faster" and "step 5.2% slower" is the
diagnosis:

* **The launch count, not the arithmetic, is what the trainer pays.** Per GDN call the chunked path issues 272 kernels
  (forward) and 680 (backward) — 16 and 40 per chunk iteration × 17 iterations (16 backward GEMMs, six elementwise
  kernels, the dO gather, the ckpt load, and the forward's 16 for the recompute) — so a training step (two forward passes
  for design §6.2's recompute plus one backward, 18 layers) is ~22,000 launches it did not have before. At ~2.7 µs of
  CPU-side cuBLAS/launch cost each that is the whole +60 ms. `test_gdn`'s per-call timings hide most of it because the
  CPU runs ahead inside a single call.
* **The FLOP count is the forward's problem.** Per chunk instance the forward is 7 GEMMs = 10.5 MFLOP and the backward
  16 + the 7 of its recompute = 32.5 MFLOP, against the sequential kernel's ~65 kFLOP per (token, head) = 5.3 GFLOP per
  call. With the padded grid (17 chunk slots × 128 sequences for N=5094, of which 62% are real) that is 22.8 GFLOP of
  forward and 70.7 GFLOP of backward per call — 4.3x and 13.3x the sequential kernel, because a chunk turns an O(1)
  per-token recurrence into an O(C) intra-chunk one.
* **Why it cannot be rescued with tensor cores here.** The 1e-4 gate (plan M3.2, and I did not loosen it) rules out
  TF32 (5e-4) and bf16 (8e-3), so these GEMMs run on `CUBLAS_COMPUTE_32F` and reach ~5–7 TFLOPS of the shape's practical
  ceiling. Design §8.3's "the fla chunked algorithm (WY form, GEMM-based) is faster" is therefore **not true for this
  kernel at this precision on this hardware**: it is true of the *backward* (1.3–1.55x) and of the layer at a uniform
  row length (1.16x), and false of the forward (3.1–3.3x slower) and of the step as the trainer runs it (+5.2%).

Consequences I took, rather than leaving a regression in the default path:

* `gdn_chunked_enabled()` requires `JT_GDN_CHUNKED=1`, so the trainer's default path is the sequential kernels,
  bit-identical to before (M6.1/M6.2 above are the same numbers M6 records). The chunked path is one environment
  variable away, and `test_gdn` exercises both in every run.
* Both implementations stay in the tree, as the design asks for the ref/kernel pair, and the reason for the default is
  written into `gdn.h` next to the switch.

What a follow-up would have to do to make this a win (all outside this task's scope, and a reason *not* to have thrown
the code away):

1. **Cut the launches.** The state-independent half (gates + 3 gathers, KK, QQ, the masks, `T = (I+L)^{-1}`) does not
   depend on the running state, so it can be done for *all* chunks of all rows in one batched pass before the chunk loop:
   5 launches instead of 5 × 17, and the 64×64 inverse for all 2176 chunks in one grid instead of 17 launches of 128
   blocks. That is ~85 launches per forward call instead of 272.
2. **Skip the padded chunk slots** (38% of them at `{1026,520,670,300,811,455,610,702}`) by compacting the active
   sequences per chunk index — a permutation plus an active count, which buys back most of the 1.6x.
3. **One GEMM for K S_0 and Q S_0** (stack K and Q into a single 128×128 operand; same FLOPs, half the calls) and the
   same for `dQ`/`dK`'s `S_0ᵀ` products.
4. Only then is it worth asking for a **G-* card run**: on the shim the sequential kernels are 8.4x slower than on
   gpubox (M7.3: 8.94 s/step vs 1.06) while cuBLAS is the strong path, so the GEMM-to-kernel ratio there is a different
   measurement from the one above — and it is the one the plan actually predicted a win for. **Unmeasured here**
   (standing rule 4: no card runs from this task).

## 4. Notes for the docs (outside this task's files)

Design §8.3's last paragraph ("The fla chunked algorithm (WY form, GEMM-based) is faster and is the M8
optimisation") needs the qualification from §3: it is faster in its backward and at a uniform row length, slower in its
forward and +5.2%/step in the trainer at fp32-exact, and it cannot use TF32 because of the 1e-4 gate. Plan M8 item 2
("Big win for the shim, where tinycublas GEMMs are the strong path") remains a plausible, still-unmeasured expectation
for the card. I did not edit either document (`docs/` is not in this task's file list; standing rule 2).

## 5. Loose ends, honestly

* No card run, so the shim/card half of the plan's expectation is unmeasured (rule 4).
* The T-inverse is the simple one-column-per-thread substitution. It is not what makes the path slow (17 launches of 128
  blocks cost ~70 µs per direction against the 4.5 ms forward), but the blocked 16×16 + GEMM form would be the next step
  if §3's follow-up 1 is done.
* The arena is the backward's `sp` buffer (`B*H*1,048,576` floats), 4.2x what the chunked path needs
  (`B*H*250,112`, `gdn_chunk_arena_floats()`); a caller with a smaller buffer gets the sequential kernels and one
  stderr line, never wrong numbers.
